# 2.2 and 2.3: a critical review of the plan (2026-11-16, for the owner's design review)

Asked by the owner on 11-16 after 2.1d: "same kind of review you did with 2.1 ... Review critically, don't just agree with something
because it's there." This reviews [README.md](README.md) (the design of 11-14; 2.3 is folded into it) against what 2.1 turned out to be
and against the record. Nothing here is decided or built.

## The short version

1. **The goal stands.** His list of goals should grow to the plays the owner named, each with its weapon and, where it is a shot, its
   trigger. The decisions of 11-14 on *form* are sound and I would keep them: a new play is a row of the choice he already makes, the
   weapon and trigger are sums the hands can overrule, one new play a step, the hands stay reactive, every sum regularized.
2. **The plan under that goal rests on three things that the record says are not true yet.** Each sank an earlier attempt.
3. **The step order is backwards on the evidence.** "Hold a spot" is first and has the worst case; "deny at an item at its time" has
   the best and is the owner's central example.
4. **2.1 gave 2.2 something the plan does not use yet**: a play can now be tried for a whole trip. And it gave 2.2 a problem the plan
   does not know about: the pull suppresses any new row.
5. **My proposal for the first step**: one play, "cover an item at its time", opened by whole-trip trials. Odds and weak points below.

## The three legs that are not there

**1. The hands cannot carry out a play they are told.** 1.7a: "The order was judged before the hands could carry it out"; on a spam order
"he goes to the spot and does not fire". 1.9a: hands "doing" 6 to 10%. 2.1a's drills: 7.8% "doing". No per-play figure exists. The README
does put drills first (decision 6 i), which is right, but it has no pass mark for them and no measurement of the hands since 2.1a.

**2. His world does not pay for these plays, and for one of them a perfect player loses.** The rule written in 2.1's own design
(INSTRUCTION.md): "A verb whose perfect script loses to free play cannot be learnt by drills: that is then the finding, and it is
fixed first." The findings were made and not fixed:

| Play | What was measured | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Hold a spot with the rail | a scripted hold with perfect aim against free play (DESIGN.md, 2.1a's probe) | −20.4 (perfect aim), −9.4 (his own aim), free play −3.2 to +2.5. "The hold does not pay yet with his present hands under either model" |
| Spam a point | scripted rockets at holding places | about level with free play (+1.6 against +1.4); only 44% of moments had a line to the point |
| **Deny an item at its time** | **never measured** | the README states "Denial shots do pay against his copies" with no measurement behind it |
| Cut off, break off | never measured (no scripted seat built) | |

Why the hold loses is in the 2.0b postmortem: "his world has no one in it who punishes a bad place"; his opponents hold the lightning 65%
of the time and the rail 0.35%, and nobody walks into a held angle the way a person does.

**3. The method for opening a play (the forecast from forced moments) does not work**, as above.

The assumption all the failed attempts shared (1.7a's order, 1.7b, 1.9a, 2.1a's instruction): *a list of plays is designed first, the head
is given the list, and the play is expected to become worth choosing.* Each time the play was not worth choosing, because the hands could
not do it, or the world did not pay for it, or it was never carried out long enough to find out (1.9a changed its order on 95% of ticks).

## What 2.1 changed for 2.2

**For it: a play can now be tried for a whole trip.** The held draw picks a goal and keeps it until the trip ends. Before 2.1, a new
choice was re-drawn five times a second, so no play was ever carried through and nothing could be learnt about it. Now a new row, once
drawn, is held: he actually goes there and does it, and the result is credited to that decision (the head's 12-second credit, which settled
its forecast error from 0.45 to 0.27 in 2.1c). This replaces the forks. A play is learnt from playing it, on his own states, which is
also the lesson of b1.3a.

**Against it: the pull shuts out any new row.** The pull adds 2 x log(the plan's odds) to each goal, with a floor at 0.01. A new row has
plan odds near zero, so it starts about 9 points down, and the plan only learns to give it odds from his own fast odds for it, which the
pull is holding down. A row opened by a small bias, the usual way, would never be drawn. **A new play needs trial trips given to it on
purpose**: a small share of his trips, set by a dial, on which the new row is the goal. That is the same coin the held draw used to come
in, and it is rule 13's "plays a small share".

**Also against it: every row needs a trip end.** The held draw ends a trip when the item is reached or gone. "Hold a spot" has no such
end, which is how a free "hold here" became camping in v0.4.3 (31 lightning pickups in 4.9 minutes). A play must come with what ends it.

## The step order, against the evidence

| Play | Do his copies make it pay? | Can the hands do it? | A natural end? | The record |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Deny / cover an item at its time** | **likely yes**: his copies take the mega a median 0.8 s after its spawn and the red 1.2 s (1.7a u90). The arrival is a known time and place (DUEL_STRATEGY principle 5: "the out-of-control player's best chance to deal damage"; 5.4: "if the timers make his enemy's arrival predictable, spam at the item pays by itself") | going to a spot: yes. Looking at a point and firing with nobody in view: not shown (1.7a: "does not fire") | **yes: the item's clock** | not measured |
| Break off | yes: fights exist, and dying is paid | it is movement | yes: out of contact | not measured |
| Cut off | depends on the brain's forecast of his path (pass odds Brier 0.080 against 0.186: usable) | movement | yes: contact or not | not measured |
| **Hold a spot watching a point** | **no evidence, and a perfect hold loses** | not shown | **no** | −20.4 / −9.4; camping in v0.4.3 and 1.9a |

The README's order is hold, deny, cut off, break off, argued from what is easiest to build ("holding needs only what 2.1 gives"). On what
is known, hold is the play least likely to work and the most likely to turn into camping, and deny is the one with a case.

**Hold and deny are one play with different weapons.** A rail held on the mega's approach in the seconds before it spawns *is* a denial
with a hitscan weapon. Seen that way, the first play is "cover item I from spot S around its time T", the weapon recommendation chooses
rockets or rail, and "hold" needs no row of its own until there is an opponent who walks into angles away from the items.

## The head's pay

There is a gap between the standing instruction and what runs. The owner, 11-11: the head "should absolutely not be paid for doing
damage"; the commands from 2.0e to 2.1e all pay the head its whole pay, the damage in it (`--head2-pay all`), and 2.1's success came
under that pay. I am not proposing to change it inside the first step of 2.2, for three reasons: the judge cannot see items and would
misprice exactly the item plays; win-chance-only for the whole network failed twice (v0.5.1 passive, v0.6.1 no lift); and one new thing
per round. But it should be a decision the owner takes knowingly, not a drift. If he wants it, its place is after a judge re-fitted on
games with items in it, as its own step.

## The trigger and the weapon

- The head's weapon scores have never been shown to win games (1.5a: 108-92 with them, 100-100 without, "within the noise").
- The head's fire push did nothing in 1.7a and "had no lesson of its own".
- The README's item 5 teaches the trigger from frozen moments, the method that has not worked.

A fork for the owner. He decided on 11-14 that the trigger is a sum of the head's score and the hands'. I would keep the sum but change
where the learning comes from: on a cover trip the **hands are shown the point** (the seven "firing solution on the point" columns
already exist in the instruction's inputs), and their own fire key learns to shoot it because the shot is paid as any damage is. The
head's part is then the decision to cover and the weapon to bring, which is what the owner described ("the head makes a decision saying
you better shoot a rocket there"), and the head's trigger score is a small helper, not the only source of a shot the hands "have no
idea" about. The alternative, the head's trigger as the main source, needs a lesson nobody has made work.

## The map sense and the slow half

If 2.1e's slow half earns its place, slow information (the map sense, item times, who is in control) should enter **the slow half**, read
at its ticks, not the fast chooser that 2.0b destabilised. If it does not earn its place, the map sense waits behind the first play: it
is not a precondition for covering an item, whose place and time he already has.

An early caution from 2.1e (six updates in): the slow halves are ticking mostly on "he changed his mind" (39 of 65 ticks a seat-minute
for the 2-second half), so they are not yet slow. 2.2's long plays need a part that really is. To be read at update 20.

## What I would propose as 2.2's first step (for the review; not decided)

**One new play: cover an item at its time.** Rows for the big items (mega and red), each a spot with a line to the item.

1. **Before any run, the rule that is already written**: a scripted perfect cover (be at the spot with rockets, fire at the item as it
   spawns, leave) against free play, on 2.1e's hands, with his copies as the opponent. One measurement of about an hour on recorded
   moments. If a perfect cover loses, the play is not learnable and the step changes; if it wins, its size is the mark the learnt play
   is read against. This is not a test in place of an idea: it is the gate the 2.1 design set and then skipped.
2. **The hands are drilled** on trial trips to go to the spot and look at the item with nobody in view, with a pass mark (the share of
   trial trips on which the shot is fired at the time).
3. **Trial trips at a small share** (a live dial, opened slowly): the cover row is drawn for him on that share of trips and held to its
   end; the trip ends on the item's clock. The head's credit learns what a cover trip is worth; the plan learns to forecast it.
4. **The row is then left to the draw**: the trial share falls and the row lives or dies on its own score.
5. The weapon recommendation for the row opens with it. The head's pay as now.

Guards from the README kept, plus two the owner asked for: **how his pickups spread over the items** and **time near one item with the
enemy out of view** (camping), and **time on a goal he is not closing on** (wandering); and frags and damage a minute first of all.

**My odds, plainly.** That a perfect cover beats free play against his copies: about 60%. That, given that, he learns to make the shot on
trial trips: about 55%. That the row then survives on its own and the checks do not fall: about 40%. That it shows as a gain against
the control within one run: about 25%; I would expect the first gain to show against people, who also walk to items on time, before it
shows against his copies. The README's first play (hold a spot) I would put under 20% on the same questions.

## The weak points of this proposal

- It still starts from a play we chose, not one he found. The trial share and the scripted probe are the guards against the shared failure.
- Copies punish a known cover spot by not coming, and the play may then pay less than the probe says. That is a correct equilibrium
  (principle 7: "stay unpredictable"), but it means the measured gain can fade.
- A cover trip is time not spent taking items himself. The item's clock bounds it; the camping guard reads it.
- The lightning habit (about 60% in hand) is untouched; a rocket denial asks him to hold rockets when he does not.
- Nothing here makes "hold an angle away from items" learnable. That needs an opponent who walks into angles (a league seat or a person),
  which is parked in the README under "After 2.2" and may have to move forward.
- The server side: the plugins never call `step()`, so the clock and the trip end of a cover row must be set by the plugin (README item 10).

## For the design review: the questions to settle

1. Is "cover an item at its time" the first play, with hold folded into it by the weapon?
2. Trial trips as the way a new row opens (a share on a dial), replacing the forecasts from forced moments?
3. Where the shot is learnt: the hands shown the point (my proposal), or the head's trigger as the main source?
4. The head's pay: leave as it runs (the whole pay) for this step, and take up win-chance-only as its own step after a judge that sees items?
5. The map sense: into the slow half if 2.1e supports it, otherwise after the first play?
6. An opponent who walks into angles: when?
