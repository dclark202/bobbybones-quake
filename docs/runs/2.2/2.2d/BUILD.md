# 2.2d: the build's record

Written by the build (an Opus 5.5 worker, 2026-11-18) for the planning session. The card is [CARD.md](CARD.md); this file is what was built,
how it was tested, and where the build decided what the card and the brief left open. Built on `main` (no worktree), not pushed. **The run has
not started**: the planning session starts it with `start.ps1`.

## Read first

1. **Every dropped pay is exactly 0.0, on every seat of every frame**, not small: proven on constructed cases, on a whole random run (8 matches x
   3,000 frames: 0 non-zero entries in `contest`, `contest_sec`, `rail_line`, `near_miss`) and on the start's own games (ten maps, 1.2 million
   seat frames: sum, size, minus and plus all `0.0`). Nothing contradicts the card.
2. **The contest counters needed a code change to run with the pay at zero.** With only `CONTEST_W=0 CEDE_W=0` the pay is zero, but three
   counters break: the steals are counted only when a steal pays (they would read 0), the frames by the second only when they pay (0), and "the
   cap reached" would count every pressured frame (the zero pay reads as a capped one). So the build adds `CONTEST_LOG=1` to `sim/duel_env.py`
   (logging only: no transfer is computed or written; the steal and the frame by the second are counted on the event; the pay columns and the
   cap stay 0). Off = bit-identical (below). `CONTEST_W` and `CEDE_W` are set to 0 as well, so the command and the live file say what plays.
3. **The weapon-choice-speed alarm's figure was missing because of the command, not the gate.** The trainer measures `kl_heads` only on its
   first 20 updates and every `--kl-heads`-th after (`update % a.kl_heads == 0 or update <= 20`), and 2.2c had `--kl-heads 10`: in 2.2c's
   metrics it is null on 21 to 29, 31 to 39, ... and present on 30, 40, 50, 60, 70. The token gate is unrelated. Fixed with `--kl-heads 1` in
   2.2d's command (read only, under `no_grad`; the learning is untouched; about 5 s more on a 365 s learning step in 2.2c's own timings). Reproduced
   and shown fixed on two 23-update CPU runs (below).
4. **The start is bit-identical to 2.2c's start file** (209 of 209 arrays equal: the dry run's `policy.pt` against
   `duel_gru_2_2c/keep/policy_start_22c.pt`), so the win scales were measured on 2.2c's start file with 2.2d's pay before the dry run.
5. **What the new log lines already show at the start** (the start playing itself, ten maps): at the enemy's free big pickups he had a clear
   line to the item at **47 of 1,324** (mean distance 733 units; without a line 1,229, mean 1,130; dead 48). And in 1.5 s windows from a
   sighting beyond 768 units, a rail would have dealt 24,960 possible against 5,072 he dealt (beyond 1,200: 1,280 against 49). A measurement
   with its fault (it assumes he would aim the same with the rail in hand), never a target.
6. **The checks fall behind the run from update 50** (a check takes about 65 minutes, ten updates): the watcher now queues them, none skipped.
   The update-60 check against 2.1d's end lands about update 80, the update-70 and the end checks after the trainer has stopped (around or
   after 08:00).

### Line A: what a rail would have dealt against what he dealt (`RAIL_WOULD`)

From a frame the enemy is in his sight (`self.visible`: the simulator's own sight, as it was when he chose the frame's action) and no window
open for his seat, a window of 60 frames (1.5 s). In it: **one possible rail hit of 80** (`W_DMG[RG]`) if at any frame his crosshair (his view
that frame, from his eye) crosses the enemy's body with a clear line: **the rail's own hit test** (the box test first, then the world traced
along the ray to 8,192 units with the sight mask, then the box test on the traced segment, as the hitscan fire code does); against **the damage
he dealt the enemy in the window** (every weapon, the points as booked per attacker). Banded by the two players' places at the window's start:
under 500, 500 to under 768, 768 to under 1,200, 1,200 and over; split by whether he owned the rail then (`has[i, RG]`). Policy seats, not
runs, the counted seat (as the rail's counters). No window opens while either player is dead. "Did the simulator already have a crosshair-on-
enemy truth": it has `stats["on_target"]` (a 4,000-unit segment against the box, no world trace, only while visible); I used the rail's own
test instead, because it has the clear line and the rail's range.

### Line B: his line to the item at the enemy's free big pickups (`FREE_SEE`)

At each enemy pickup of the mega or an armor where his pressure was 0 (the contest counters' "free pickup"), a point trace from his eye to the
item's place + 16 units up (the contest pay's own trace target): with a line / without, his distance from the item (the counters' own), the
mean of each; a dead seat (or one waiting to respawn) counted apart.

## Read only: 2.2c's pull's proof (the brief's D)

No fix. From `duel_gru_2_2c/metrics.jsonl` (`pull.worst`): 11 updates over 0.003, one over 0.01 (update 25: 0.0139). Each is **one frame or a
few of the 10,752 sampled** (over 0.003: 1 to 5 frames; over 0.01: 1, at update 25 only); the 99.9th percentile under 0.0024 every time. The
worst frames share nothing visible: 10 of 11 not at a reading, none at a reset or a round's edge, the target varies (3 to 12), the played log-
probability from -0.45 to -4.5. Two of them (updates 25, the failure, and 53) were at the same step of the rollout (t = 150) with a new draw
and a plan refresh on both seats; the rest had neither. **2.2b, before the lever existed, shows the same**: 15 of 99 updates over 0.003, its
largest 0.0181 (update 47, one frame), one at t = 150 too. So it is not 2.2c's lever; it reads as rare single-frame numerical differences
between play and learning, with a possible link to a fresh draw at a plan refresh that two frames cannot settle.

## The start

```
powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File docs\runs\2.2\2.2d\start.ps1 -Minutes <minutes from now to 08:00 on 11-19> -Updates 80
```
`-Minutes` must be over 60; `-Updates` over 80 is refused. It refuses if `data/sim_runs/duel_gru_2_2d/policy.pt` exists (it does not).
